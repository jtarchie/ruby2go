# Decision 390

**Rule:** `Float#**` is correctly rounded; a table-driven log/exp and a double-double square-and-multiply for integer exponents up to 64 answer when no rounding midpoint lies within their error bound plus the exact series' own, so they return exactly what the series would, and the series runs only for the rest.

**Fast `Float#**`, the same digits** (#134; 2026-10-26). *See also decisions 43* (this is its exception: `**` is not Go's `math.Pow`), *387* (which found the cost). Float#** has been correctly rounded since ed75fa26 (no decision then): `rbFloatPowExact` (`prelude/go/floatpow.go`) evaluates exp(y·log|x|) by double-double series to under 2^-84 and rounds once, about 820 instructions a call, because Go's `math.Pow` is often an ulp or more off (`8.0 ** (1.0/3)` is 1.9999999999999998, MRI 2.0). spinel's bm_partial_sums spent 2,050M of its 2,644M instructions there.

- **Paths.** `rbFloatPow` keeps the special cases (`y == 2` is `x*x`, as MRI; zeros, ±1, NaN, Inf and a negative base with a fractional exponent go to `math.Pow`). Then:
  - **Integer y, |y| <= 64:** `rbPowInt`, square-and-multiply in double-double (error under 2^-98), a reciprocal for negative y. It declines a result outside [2^-900, 2^900], where the products' low words could go subnormal.
  - **Everything else:** `rbPowFast`, musl's shape: |x| = 2^k·z, a 128-entry table of 1/c (8 significant bits, so r = z/c − 1 is exact) and log c as a double-double, log1p(r) by Taylor to r^11, summed as a double-double with a computed absolute error bound (`errL`); then exp by a 128-entry table of 2^(j/128) and a degree-7 polynomial. The tables come from `go -C scripts/pow-tables run .` (math/big, 300 bits).
  - **Rounding:** `rbPowRound(h, lo, err)` widens the bound by 2^-75 (the series' own error is under 2^-84) and rounds only if h + (lo − err) and h + (lo + err) round to the same double. Then the true value and the series' value both lie inside an interval holding no midpoint, so both round to that double: the result is identical to the series', not merely correctly rounded. An exact midpoint (`x**3` of a 19-bit x can be one) always falls back.
  - **Out of range:** the series hands a result past (-708.39, 709.79) to `math.Pow`. A first estimate y·(k·ln2 + log c + r), off by under |y|·2^-13, catches |p| > 760 before the series; `rbPowEdge` then returns ±0 or ±Inf (sign by an odd integer y and a negative base), which is what `math.Pow` returns there (its squaring loop gives up past exponent 4096 and `Ldexp` under- or overflows). Between 708 and 760 it calls `math.Pow` as the series did, or the series itself within 0.005 of the series' cutoffs.
- **Checked.** A scratch harness (built from a generated program, as `TestFloatPowFastPaths` is) compared `rbFloatPow` with the former body (special cases, then `rbFloatPowExact`) on 60M pairs in 12 kinds (random bit patterns, x in [2^-10, 2^10] with y in [-100, 100], integer y in [-64, 64] with either sign of x, y = n + 0.5, |p| within 0.02 of 708, 709, 708.39, 709.79, 745.13 and 760, x within 2^-52 of 1 with |p| < 700, subnormal x, `(2/3)**k`): 0 differ. Against 256-bit `math/big`, on 2.9M of them the largest error was 7% of the bound. Shrinking the bound by 2^20 made 1-55 per 2M differ, so the comparison has teeth; `TestFloatPowFastPaths` keeps 1.2M pairs and fails on the same mutation. Fast-path coverage: 99.5-99.99% of in-range calls per kind (the rest are near-midpoints), the integer path 97% of its calls (the rest out of its range).
- **MRI is not correctly rounded.** #134 assumed MRI's `pow` is. On 2.4M of these pairs, MRI 4.0 on macOS differs from rugaroo on 2,335 in-range results, and in every one rugaroo is the correctly rounded value (checked with `math/big`): macOS's libm misrounds about 1 in 700 there; glibc's pow is documented at 0.52 ulp, so Linux misrounds some too. Exactly MRI's digits would mean porting each platform's libm (decision 43's reasoning). So the minitest checks are ones every correct-to-an-ulp pow agrees on: exact powers (b^k below 2^53 for b in 2..40, either sign, 2^±k to the subnormal end), large exponents, and the ±0/±Inf edges.
- **Not done: subnormal results.** `math.Pow`'s results in the subnormal range differ from MRI in 18,019 of 46,384 sampled (in 3,262 of them MRI is not correctly rounded either). Unchanged here, since the fast paths must equal the former path; rounding them at 2^-1074 (musl's exp specialcase: add 1.0 to the scaled value first) is the fix, as a follow-up with its own check.
- **Cost** (instructions, `GOMAXPROCS=1`, median of 5): bm_partial_sums 2,644M to 938M (0.43 s to 0.078 s; spinel 0.028 s, 316M). Its loop without `**` is 623M, so `**` is now 126 instructions a call there (nearly all calls underflow: `(2/3) ** (d-1)` past d = 1,748), and the rest is `Math.sin`/`cos` and division. A loop of 2M fractional and 2M small-integer exponents: 3,422M to 836M (about 830 to 180 instructions a call). `benchmarks/math.rb` has no `**`: 51.9M both ways. `TestCompileCost`'s three programs do not reach `Float#**`, so their generated code is unchanged; a program that does gains four functions and 5 KB of tables.
- **Proof:** `TestFloatPowFastPaths` (`floatpow_test.go`); `test_float_pow_exact_powers` and `test_float_pow_large_exponents` in `testdata/test/number_test.rb`, against MRI.
